## Product and infrastructure positioning

- Feature inventory: `https://www.cyberghostvpn.com/features`
- NoSpy: `https://www.cyberghostvpn.com/features/nospy`
- Dedicated IP: `https://www.cyberghostvpn.com/dedicated-ip-vpn`
- Android guide: `https://support.cyberghostvpn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360006573873-How-to-Use-CyberGhost-VPN-on-Android`
- Windows Smart Rules: `https://support.cyberghostvpn.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016968679-How-to-Define-Smart-Rules-for-CyberGhost-VPN-on-Windows`
- Platform support: `https://support.cyberghostvpn.com/hc/en-us/articles/213898525-What-Devices-and-Platforms-Do-You-Support`

Current first-party positioning includes:

- 120+ locations in 100 countries and up to seven devices;
- specialized streaming, P2P and gaming servers;
- RAM-only servers, automatic Wi-Fi protection, content blocker, DNS-leak protection, kill switch and split tunneling;
- OpenVPN, WireGuard and IKEv2 where each platform supports them;
- native or guided routes for Windows, macOS, Android, iOS, Linux CLI, Android/Google TV, Fire TV, Smart DNS, consoles and routers.

Android's paid experience, corroborated by the official guide, is one-tap Best Location plus Countries, Streaming and Favorites tabs. Streaming entries are purpose-specific rather than just country labels. Favorites work for countries, cities and streaming destinations. Dedicated IP is a separate purchase activated with a token and then appears under Favorites.

NoSpy is a premium infrastructure story: CyberGhost says these servers are owned and managed end-to-end inside its Romanian headquarters with an internal team and dedicated uplink. It separately describes self-owned, self-controlled colocated hardware in third-party data centers. These are vendor claims, not independently tested by this runtime pass.

Dedicated IP uses a privacy-shaped recovery trade-off: CyberGhost says the address is bound to a random token rather than the account, the server-side token is wiped after redemption, and the user must retain the token for future installs. If it is lost, it cannot be recovered. Current public locations listed on the product page include the US, Germany, France, UK, Australia, Belgium, Singapore, Spain, Japan, Netherlands, Sweden and Canada.

Windows goes beyond mobile automation. `Smart Rules` can connect on launch with a delay, launch an app after connection, react differently to secured/unsecured/saved Wi-Fi, exclude websites and automatically connect when named apps launch. This is a strong power-user layer for POKROV's later desktop roadmap.

## Pricing, trial, checkout and referrals

- Plan mechanics: `https://support.cyberghostvpn.com/hc/en-us/articles/213849009-How-do-CyberGhost-s-Plans-and-Offers-Work`
- Purchase guide: `https://support.cyberghostvpn.com/hc/en-us/articles/213650885-How-to-Purchase-a-Subscription`
- Refer a friend: `https://www.cyberghostvpn.com/refer-a-friend`
- Referral terms: `https://www.cyberghostvpn.com/ref-terms`

Observed Android in-app pricing is retained in the runtime profile: three days free then `3,590 RUB/year`, or `699 RUB/month`. Google Play payments were suspended in Russia, so no trial or purchase started.

Public website structure is one month, six months and a long introductory term that renews at the disclosed standard rate. A current first-party product page showed `$12.99/month`, a 26-month intro at `$2.19/month` billed `$56.94` then renewed annually at `$56.94`, and a six-month offer at `$6.99/month` billed `$41.94`; treat these as mutable US-session prices. Website payment routes include card, PayPal and Bitcoin and use an email to create/tie the account. App-store purchases are handled by the platform and are excluded from CyberGhost's direct money-back guarantee. CyberGhost advertises 14 days for one-month web subscriptions and 45 days for terms of six months or longer.

Referral mechanics are unusually concrete:

- up to three friends;
- both referrer and new paid subscriber receive 30 free days per qualifying referral;
- maximum referrer reward is 90 days;
- the friend must be new and purchase through the link;
- the referrer keeps earned days even if the friend cancels;
- a friend who cancels inside the refund window forfeits their own referral reward.

This is a simple double-sided retention mechanic with a hard cap, a clearer reward than generic `invite friends` copy, and low fraud surface because it requires a paid qualifying subscription.

## Privacy, telemetry and support disclosures

- Privacy policy: `https://www.cyberghostvpn.com/privacypolicy`
- Terms: `https://www.cyberghostvpn.com/terms`

CyberGhost claims it does not monitor or retain tunnel browsing history, traffic destinations/content, search preferences, DNS queries, source IP mappings, timestamps or session duration. Its privacy policy separately discloses account/payment/support/fraud data and a broad set of data it calls non-personal: browser/device/OS/language/time, battery, screen, Google Advertising ID, gyroscope, WLAN/carrier, referrer and preferences. Service events include connection attempt/success, country of origin without source IP, version and time interval. Optional diagnostics can include app data and a connection log attached to a support ticket.

Named public vendors include Google Analytics, Mouseflow and AppsFlyer for the website; Cleverbridge/Stripe/PayPal and other processors for payments; Zendesk for chat/support; and VWO/Iterable for survey flows. The privacy policy permits necessary group-company sharing and names Kape as ultimate holding company. The installed app's nested privacy screen gives separate Essential, Analytics and Marketing controls and names Google Analytics for Firebase, AppsFlyer and Firebase; Analytics and Marketing were on by default in this session and were manually disabled.

Terms cover 18+ eligibility, auto-installed updates, broad prohibited use, no availability/performance warranty, auto-renewal and cancellation/refund rules. US users get binding arbitration/class-waiver language with a 30-day opt-out. Non-US disputes point to London courts, with UK LCIA arbitration as a fallback and English law.

## Release and distribution pattern

- Android distributes through Google Play, with an official APK/sideload path for Android TV when Play is unavailable.
- iOS distributes through Apple's App Store; Fire TV uses Amazon Appstore; desktop and Linux/manual configurations are routed through the site/account Download Hub.
- Installed Android `8.40.0` and live Play update date 2026-07-14 are the current Android evidence.
- Apple history shows approximately monthly iOS releases from April through July 2026, but user-facing notes are mainly generic maintenance copy.
- The site promises regular cross-platform updates, while Terms authorize automatic update/download and allow features or servers to be added or removed.
- There is no clear public, cross-platform feature changelog. Distribution is broad and frequent, but release communication is weak.

## Competitive lessons for POKROV

- Build a real account hub: devices, downloads, manual configs, privacy tools and add-ons in one place.
- Turn server selection into jobs-to-be-done—streaming service, gaming, P2P, best location—not just a country picker.
- Pair one-tap simplicity with optional automation rules and favorites; expose the power gradually.
- Make privacy proof a product surface: quarterly request counts, independent audit scope and explicit infrastructure architecture.
- Use double-sided referral rewards with a clear cap and qualification rule.
- Keep every store creative, legal address, age rule, entitlement and cross-sell synchronized. CyberGhost's stale Private Browser creative, address/age mismatch and device-limit artifacts are avoidable trust damage.
